    RULES

    The rulebook. No taglines.

    The game

    It is snakes and ladders, except worse. Or better, if you are a masochist. The game is called very unfair snakes and ladders, or vusal.

    Get to tile 100 first. If you ever land on 3, then 7, then 13, in that order on separate turns, you win. A few cards also make you win outright.

    Cards that explicitly say you win, you lose or the game ends resolve automatically, including on the first turn. The game-over screen and LOG show the exact reason. VC25 blocks those instant results while it is in play.

    Setting up

    Pawns are assigned to players by number, 1 to 4. Numbers also determine turn order, and players sit clockwise according to turn order: player 1 north, player 2 east, player 3 south, player 4 west. One round is reaching (or, in some cases, surpassing) the turn of player 1.

    Everyone starts on tile 1. Put your pawn on your tile — that is how you keep track of it. Put the shuffled deck beside the board, face down, and every played card goes on the other side of the board, face up.

    Cards

    Every time you land on an even-numbered tile, draw a card. Unless the card says to play it immediately, or places an ongoing effect on you, you may hold onto it. Do not show anyone your hand or you are eliminated and your cards go back into the deck, which is reshuffled (unless you are playing against one other person — then they just win).

    If the effect works against you, you have to play the card. If the effect is ongoing and not a Good card, you cannot play it to get rid of the effect. Playing a card resolves its effects: it stops functioning entirely afterwards. For example, if you play a card that makes someone else draw two, they draw two and the card does nothing afterwards.

    There are three types, 36 of each in this version: Good (light and cold colours), Bad (dark and warm colours) and Vertigo (solely neutral colours). Vertigo cards vary so much by situation that they fit neither of the other two.

    Resolution order

    1. Resolve movement.
    2. Check snake or ladder.
    3. Check win condition.
    4. If the final tile is even and drawing is allowed, draw.
    5. Resolve any immediate card effect.
    6. Place ongoing cards into the player's hand.
    7. Update temporary and global status effects.
    8. End turn.

    Some card effects override this order. The table has the final say.

    Point Nemo

    Tiles 0 down to −9 sit underneath tile 1. You cannot draw any cards there, but every roll moves you away from −9. Land on −9 and you lose. Roll an exact number back onto zero and you escape onto tile 1. Every time it is your turn in there — including on tile 0 — it drags you back one tile before you roll, so from 0 you go to −1 and a roll of 1 escapes through zero.

    Atmosphere

    Play some jazz. Or don't. Some bossa nova would be nice, though. There is a JAZZ button in the header; it is off by default.

    House interpretations in this build

    • Snakes and ladders apply once per landing — they do not chain (17 → 14 does not then climb 14 → 40).
    • Reaching 100 normally needs an exact roll; an overshoot leaves you where you are. GC33 removes that requirement.
    • The 3 → 7 → 13 sequence advances at most once per player turn: each tile must be landed on in a different turn. The final 13 is checked before its snake.
    • Overshooting zero in Point Nemo leaves you on 0. At the start of your next turn the drag takes you to −1, so a 1 escapes through zero.
    • Bad cards play immediately on draw. Good cards stay in hand until played. Vertigo cards resolve immediately unless they are ongoing.
    • A round advances each time the turn passes player 1, including when VC18 reverses direction.
    • VC25's “no instant result” is treated as a global effect while the card is in hand.
    • GC15 mirrors the next snake's distance upward. GC19 / GC12 intercept your next Bad card. GC20 also moves you five ahead when a Bad card is used on you.

    Table calls

    Weather, Wordle, buying food, insults, compliments, fun facts, jazz and the £1 owed to the Rothschilds family are not automated. The game shows the card and asks the table to rule on it. Everything else — movement, snakes, ladders, Point Nemo, draws, hands, statuses and win conditions — is handled by the game.

    Playing online

    Create a room in the menu and share the code or link. Each player joins on their own device and is assigned a seat. Fill every selected seat; only the host starts the game, and START creates a fresh state for everyone. Each seat sees only its own hand; the deck, discard and log are shared. The host has DM controls for anything the table wants to overrule.

    On phones, same Wi‑Fi: run npm start on a computer and open http://<computer-IP>:3000 on each phone. No internet needed. If players are in different houses, use a tunnel or deploy the server to a public HTTPS URL, then share the game link; DEPLOY.md in the full-game ZIP has the steps. One phone on its own can play pass & play from the standalone HTML file, but online rooms need the server.
